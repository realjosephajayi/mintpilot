How it works

You do the setup once. MintPilot handles the timing.

  1. Add a funded wallet

    Create a MintPilot wallet or import your own, then send ETH to it on the network you want to mint on.

  2. Schedule the mint

    Send a contract address or an OpenSea link, choose how many NFTs and when. MintPilot checks the collection and warns you if it is sold out or your wallet is not on the allowlist.

  3. Get the result

    At the set time MintPilot signs and sends the transaction. You get a message with the outcome, the NFT IDs and a link to the transaction, or a plain explanation if it did not go through.

What MintPilot does

Everything between “I want that mint” and “it’s in my wallet”.

Sends at the right moment

Transactions are prepared ahead of time and sent as a mint opens. If the first try lands early, MintPilot keeps checking and tries again the moment it opens.

Handles the mints people use

OpenSea drops, public mints and allowlist mints on supported contracts. Paste a collection link or a contract address.

Checks before it spends

Sold-out collections and wallets that are not on the allowlist are flagged when you schedule or before a transaction is sent.

You set the price limit

Choose the most you are willing to pay per NFT. MintPilot refuses to send a transaction above it.

Explains what happened

Every result arrives in Telegram in plain language, with the NFT IDs and a link to the transaction.

A live view of your mints

Open a private, view-only dashboard to see upcoming mints, past results and each attempt as it happens.

Built around your limits

MintPilot signs transactions for you, so it treats your money carefully and tells you how.

  • Spending limits

    A price limit you choose for each mint, plus a built-in cap on how much a single transaction can spend.

  • Encrypted keys

    Wallet keys are encrypted at rest and used only to sign the mints you scheduled.

  • No recovery phrase, ever

    MintPilot never asks for your seed phrase. Use a dedicated wallet and fund it only with what you plan to mint with.

  • Private, expiring links

    Dashboard and wallet-import links are made just for you and expire after a short time.

Treat a MintPilot wallet like any hot wallet. Minting is never guaranteed: the project’s contract, the network and the remaining supply decide the outcome.

Questions

Do I need to keep Telegram open?

No. Once a mint is scheduled it runs on MintPilot’s servers. Telegram is only where you set things up and where the results arrive.

What does it cost?

MintPilot itself is free to start. Each mint costs the mint price plus the network fee, paid from your MintPilot wallet. Premium is optional and is paid with Telegram Stars.

Can I use my own wallet?

Yes. Import it through the private page the bot links to, or create a new wallet inside MintPilot. For either one, fund it only with what you plan to mint with.

What if the collection sells out or I am not on the allowlist?

MintPilot tells you in plain language. Sold-out collections are flagged when you schedule, and if a wallet is not eligible for the live stage nothing is sent and you are told why.

Is a mint guaranteed?

No. Speed helps, but the project’s contract, network congestion and the remaining supply decide who gets an NFT. MintPilot removes the manual timing, not the competition.

Which collections work?

OpenSea drops and mints on supported contracts across the networks listed above. If a collection does not work, send the details through /support in the bot and we will look at it.

Where do I get help?

Open the bot and send /support. Describe what you were doing and the mint number, and never include a private key or recovery phrase.
